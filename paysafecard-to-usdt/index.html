One token, six separate rails

Tether issues USDT on several blockchains. They all represent the same dollar, but they don't talk to each other: USDT sent on Tron can only be received by a Tron address. If you choose ERC-20 and paste a Tron address, or the other way round, the transfer can be lost.

So start from the receiving side. Open the wallet or exchange where you want the USDT, tap deposit or receive, choose USDT, and look at the network it offers. Select that same network in the exchange form and copy the address it shows.

Which network

  • TRC-20 (Tron): low fees, very widely used on exchanges.
  • BEP-20 (BNB Chain): cheap, convenient for Binance users.
  • ERC-20 (Ethereum): needed for Ethereum apps; higher fees later.
  • Solana, TON, Polygon: if your wallet lives on those chains.

Sending to Binance, Bybit or another exchange

Exchanges accept USDT on several networks and give a separate deposit address for each. Choose the network on the exchange's deposit page first, then copy the address it generates. TRC-20 and BEP-20 deposits are typically credited after a few confirmations; ERC-20 deposits take a little longer.

Fees after you receive

Moving USDT later costs a network fee paid in the chain's native coin: TRX on Tron, ETH on Ethereum, BNB on BNB Chain, SOL, TON or POL. A wallet holding only USDT can receive it but can't send it on until it holds a little of that coin. Exchanges cover this for you when you withdraw.

USDT questions

Which network is cheapest?

For receiving, all are fine. For moving it on, Tron, BNB Chain, Solana, TON and Polygon cost a fraction of Ethereum.

Can I receive USDT from a euro voucher?

Yes. The euro value is converted to USDT at the rate shown in the form, fixed when you request the payment link.

How do I check the transfer?

Search your address on the explorer for the network: Tronscan, Etherscan, BscScan, Solscan, Tonviewer or Polygonscan.

What if I chose the wrong network?

Contact support before paying if you notice it. Once sent, a transfer to an address on another network may not be recoverable.

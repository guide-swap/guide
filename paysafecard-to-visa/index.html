What happens on the card side

A payout to a card is a push payment: money is sent to the account behind the card, rather than taken from it. That's why the card number is enough, and why nobody ever needs the expiry date or the three-digit security code to pay you.

How quickly it appears depends on the issuing bank. Digital banks such as Revolut, N26 or Monzo usually show card credits within minutes. Traditional banks can take up to a working day to post them.

Cards that work

  • Debit, credit and most prepaid Visa or Mastercard cards.
  • Virtual cards from digital banks.
  • Some prepaid cards cap incoming payments; check your card's limits for larger amounts.

Match the currency to the card

A euro card that receives dollars will be converted by your bank at its own rate, often with a foreign-transaction fee. Choosing the currency the card account is held in avoids that. A card from a UK bank in pounds can still receive EUR; the bank will convert it, so for GBP cards it can be better to pick a different route, such as Revolut or a bank transfer.

Card payout questions

Is it safe to enter my card number?

Yes. A card number on its own lets someone send money to the card, not take it. Never share the expiry date, CVV or one-time codes.

Why hasn't the money appeared yet?

Some banks show card credits only after they post them, which can take until the next working day. If nothing shows after 24 hours, contact support with the order number.

Can I use a credit card?

Yes. The payout reduces the card's outstanding balance or adds a credit to it, depending on the bank.

Which currencies can be paid to a card?

EUR and USD.

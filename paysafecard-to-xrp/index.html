The destination tag

Exchanges such as Binance, Kraken or Bitstamp use one shared XRP address for many customers and tell deposits apart by a number called the destination tag. If you send to an exchange without the tag, the XRP arrives at the exchange but not in your account, and recovering it means a support ticket. Your own wallet doesn't need a tag.

Copy both the address and the tag from the exchange's XRP deposit page and enter both in the form.

Two questions before ordering

  • Is the address on an exchange? Then the tag is mandatory.
  • Is it a brand-new wallet? Then the first payment must cover the reserve.
  • XRP addresses start with r.

The account reserve

A new XRP Ledger account is activated by its first incoming payment, and part of the balance stays locked as a reserve to stop spam accounts. The base reserve is currently 1 XRP. Send at least that much to a new self-custody wallet; a smaller first payment can't activate it. Exchange accounts don't have this concern, because the exchange's address is already active.

XRP questions

I forgot the destination tag. What now?

Contact the exchange's support with the transaction hash and your account details. They can usually credit it manually, but it takes time.

Which wallet should I use?

Xaman (formerly Xumm) is the most common self-custody app. Ledger hardware wallets also support XRP.

Is the reserve lost?

No, it stays in your wallet. It just can't be spent while the account exists.
